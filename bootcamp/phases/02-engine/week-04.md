## 📐 CONCEPT INJECTIONS

### 📐 CONCEPT — Slippage and market impact

```text
📐 CONCEPT: Slippage, spread, and square-root market impact
WHY NOW:   Your break-even table (Tuesday) needs a defensible bps number, and "1 bp because that is
           my commission" is not defensible.
FORMULA:   total_cost_bps = fee_bps + (spread_bps / 2) + impact_bps
           impact_bps ~ eta * sigma * sqrt(order_size / ADV),  eta ~ 0.1
           spread_bps ~ 2*(exp(alpha)-1)/(1+exp(alpha))  [Corwin-Schultz high-low estimator]
CODE:      sig, adv = 0.20, 2_000_000
           impact = 0.1 * sig * np.sqrt(10_000 / 100 / adv)      # $10k order, $100 px
           print(f"impact {impact*1e4:.1f} bps")
EXAMPLE:   A $10,000 order in a name with $2m ADV and 20% vol costs ~14 bps of impact, on top of
           5 bps of half-spread. Your "1 bp fee" assumption understates the real cost by 19x.
           Recompute S03's break-even with 19 bps instead of 6: it dies at 118 trades/yr by a mile.
TIME:      20 min
NOTE:      write notes/concepts/slippage-models.md using Concept Note Template
DONE WHEN: you can estimate a spread from OHLC in 3 lines, explain in 2 sentences why impact is
           superlinear in size, and spot a backtest that sizes positions as a % of NAV with no
           reference to ADV (symptom: a $10m strategy and a $100m strategy print the same Sharpe)
```

### 📐 CONCEPT — Purge and embargo

```text
📐 CONCEPT: Purge and embargo in walk-forward validation
WHY NOW:   Wednesday's embargo-audit.md is blocked until you know how long the embargo must be.
FORMULA:   embargo >= max forward-looking window used by any feature or label
           purge: delete training rows whose LABEL window overlaps the test window
           A label at time t covering [t, t+h] overlaps test start T if t + h >= T
CODE:      lo = idx.searchsorted(test_start); hi = idx.searchsorted(test_end, side="right")
           drop = range(max(lo - embargo, 0), min(hi + embargo, len(idx)))
           train_purged = idx.drop(idx[list(drop)])
EXAMPLE:   S06 (time-series momentum) labels each bar with the NEXT 21 days' return. With a 1-bar
           embargo, 20 of the last training rows have seen 20 of the first test rows. Purging
           20 bars at every boundary drops S06's OOS Sharpe from 0.9 to 0.4 — a strategy you had
           mentally shipped is now borderline.
TIME:      20 min
NOTE:      write notes/concepts/purge-and-embargo.md using Concept Note Template
DONE WHEN: you can compute the required embargo from a label definition in 3 lines, explain in
           2 sentences why a shorter embargo makes OOS optimistic, and spot a split where the
           embargo was applied to the TEST set instead of the TRAIN set
```

### 📐 CONCEPT — Survivorship bias

```text
📐 CONCEPT: Survivorship bias
WHY NOW:   Friday's universe audit. You chose tickers that exist today and backtested them from 2015.
FORMULA:   bias ~ E[return of survivors] - E[return of the full point-in-time universe]
           Rule of thumb: index-level survivorship inflates returns ~0.5-1.5%/yr; single-name
           "top 10 by market cap today" universes can inflate by 3%+/yr.
CODE:      pit = pit_membership(asof="2015-01-01")     # who was in the universe THEN
           bias = run(px[col for col in today_members]).cagr - run(px[pit]).cagr
EXAMPLE:   Your 5-symbol universe (AAPL, SPY, IWM, QQQ, XLK) is all index products still trading
           today. Every one of them survived 2015-2025. The bias is real but bounded (<1%/yr)
           because they are diversified products, not single names. A universe of "today's top 20
           S&P stocks" would be far worse. State the number, do not hand-wave.
TIME:      15 min
NOTE:      write notes/concepts/survivorship-bias.md using Concept Note Template
DONE WHEN: you can describe a point-in-time universe in 3 lines, explain in 2 sentences why
           today's membership is not 2015's, and spot a backtest whose universe list was built
           by screening on a metric computed at the END of the sample
```
